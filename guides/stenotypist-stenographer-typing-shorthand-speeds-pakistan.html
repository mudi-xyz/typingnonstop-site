Stenographer shorthand and typing speeds in Pakistan

Stenotypist and stenographer posts in Pakistan test two separate skills: shorthand, where you write dictated speech in symbols and then transcribe it, and typing. Candidates often prepare only for one, and the second part then costs them the job.

Speeds seen in adverts

For the KPPSC case, see the KPPSC stenographer guide.

How the tests usually work

  • Shorthand test. A passage is dictated at the required speed and you write it in shorthand.
  • Transcription. You read back or type your notes within a time limit. Accuracy matters because errors reduce your marks.
  • Typing test. A separate passage is typed in a set time at the required speed.

Why typing needs its own practice

Strong shorthand does not guarantee a strong typing score. At 40 WPM with a high accuracy requirement, a few typing mistakes can drop your net result below the pass mark. Net WPM counts correct words only, as explained in net versus gross WPM.

A balanced practice plan

  • Daily shorthand. Practise dictation at a speed a little above your comfort level with a partner, a recording or a teacher.
  • Daily typing. Fifteen minutes of timed copy typing, using a simple routine.
  • Weak words. Review the words you miss most often with weak-word drills.
  • Weekly mock test. One test that includes both parts, timed as in the advert.

Frequently asked questions

What speed does a stenotypist need in Pakistan?

One Power Division advert for Stenotypist BPS-14 asked for 80 WPM English shorthand and 40 WPM typing. Other adverts differ, so read yours.

Are shorthand and typing tested separately?

Yes. Adverts list the two speeds separately, and each is usually tested as its own part.

Does TypingNonStop teach shorthand?

No. It helps with typing speed and accuracy. Use a teacher or recorded dictation for shorthand practice.
