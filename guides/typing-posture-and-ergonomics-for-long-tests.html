Typing posture and ergonomics for long practice and test sessions

Typing tests can last ten minutes or more, and practice sessions often last longer. Good posture protects your hands and keeps your accuracy steady.

The basic position

Sit with your back supported and your feet flat on the floor. Keep your elbows near your sides, bent at about a right angle, and your forearms roughly level with the keyboard. Your wrists should stay in a neutral, straight position, not bent up or down.

The screen and the keyboard

Place the screen at arm’s length with the top near eye level so you do not tilt your head. Keep the keyboard directly in front of you. Do not hunch forward to read the text.

Hands and fingers

Keep your fingers curved and relaxed, hovering lightly over the keys instead of pressing hard. Heavy keystrokes tire the hand and do not make you faster. Relax your shoulders and jaw, because tension travels down the arms.

Breaks

Rest your eyes and hands every 25 to 30 minutes. Look at something distant, shake out your hands and stretch your fingers. Stop if you feel pain and speak to a health professional if it persists.

Before the test

If you can, adjust the chair and screen at the test centre before the clock starts. Take a slow breath, drop your shoulders and rest your fingers on the home row.

Frequently asked questions

Does posture affect typing speed?

It can. A comfortable, relaxed position helps you keep a steady rhythm and avoid fatigue errors.

Do I need a wrist rest?

Not necessarily. A neutral wrist position matters more than the accessory.

How often should I take breaks?

A short break every 25 to 30 minutes is a common guideline.
