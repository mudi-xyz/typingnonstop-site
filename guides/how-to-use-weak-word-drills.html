How to use weak-word drills to fix repeat typing mistakes

Most typing mistakes are repeats. A small group of words causes a large share of your errors, so the fastest way to improve is to find them and practise them directly.

Step 1: collect your mistakes

After each practice test, write down the words you typed wrongly, and what you typed instead. Many typists notice patterns: swapped letters such as teh for the, doubled letters, or one finger that always misses.

Step 2: drill a short list

Take 10 to 15 words and type each one several times in a row, then mix them in a different order. Short, repeated practice builds the correct movement and replaces the wrong habit.

Step 3: check the next day

Words that returned correctly in a test on the following day are fixed. Words that came back wrong need another round. Keep a running list and cross out the words that stay correct.

Step 4: remove the pattern, not just the word

If the same kind of error keeps appearing, such as the wrong finger on a particular key, add words with that key to your drill. You are retraining the finger, not memorising a spelling.

Frequently asked questions

How many words should I drill at once?

Ten to fifteen is a manageable number. Longer lists make practice slow and unfocused.

How often should I drill?

Ten minutes a day on your mistake list is a good routine.

Why do the same words keep coming back?

Muscle memory repeats what you practise. Drills replace the wrong movement with the right one.
