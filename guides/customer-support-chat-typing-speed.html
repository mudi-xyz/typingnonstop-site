Typing speed for customer support and live chat jobs

Live chat and email support roles depend on typing, but the goal is not raw speed. It is typing clearly and quickly enough to keep several conversations moving without errors that confuse customers.

What employers usually ask for

Postings for chat and email support tend to ask for figures in the same range as other office jobs, often about 40 to 60 words per minute, and some roles do not state a number at all. Where a typing test is part of hiring, it is usually a short copy test or a short written reply to a sample customer message. Always follow the figure in the posting you are applying to.

Why speed alone is not enough

  • Accuracy. A wrong order number, email address or amount creates extra work for everyone.
  • Tone. Short, polite, well-punctuated replies read as professional. Missing capitals and clumsy punctuation do not.
  • Multitasking. Many agents handle more than one chat, so you read, think and type in short bursts. Smooth rhythm helps more than a sprint.
  • Saved replies. Most teams use templates, so you spend your own typing on the personal parts of each message.

What to practise

  • Short messages with names and numbers. Type sentences that include order numbers, dates and email addresses.
  • Punctuation and capitals. Practise the habits in the punctuation guide until they are automatic.
  • Looking at the screen, not the keys. You need your eyes on the customer's message, as covered in how to stop looking at the keyboard.
  • Weak words. Repeated misspellings stand out in customer replies, so drill them using weak-word drills.

If there is a written reply test

Some employers give you a sample complaint and ask for a reply in a set time. Read the message once, note the key facts, then write a clear answer: greet the customer, deal with the problem, say what happens next and close politely. Read it once before sending, because one proofreading pass costs a few seconds and saves marks.

A simple routine

Practise for 15 minutes a day, as in the daily routine guide. Split it into a warm-up, a timed test at your target accuracy, and a short drill on your weak words. After two or three weeks check your net speed and accuracy again.

Common slips that stand out in customer replies

  • Mixing up its and it’s, or your and you’re.
  • Leaving out apostrophes, capital letters at the start of sentences, or the space after a full stop.
  • Typing the wrong digit in an order number or phone number.
  • Sending a half-finished sentence because you pressed Enter too early.

Most of these are habits, not knowledge gaps, which is why daily accuracy practice works better than occasional long sessions.

What to say on your CV

List your tested net speed and accuracy, plus any relevant experience such as email or chat support, and mention comfort with handling more than one conversation. A recruiter reading a support CV wants evidence that you can write clearly under time pressure, so a short, correct, well-punctuated CV is itself a typing test.

Frequently asked questions

How fast do I need to type for a chat support job?

Postings usually ask for a figure similar to other office roles, often about 40 to 60 words per minute, and some give no number. Check the exact posting.

Do chat support jobs test typing?

Some do, usually with a short copy test or a written reply to a sample customer message.

Is accuracy or speed more important in live chat?

Both matter, but accuracy and a clear tone usually matter more, because mistakes in names, numbers and amounts create extra work.
