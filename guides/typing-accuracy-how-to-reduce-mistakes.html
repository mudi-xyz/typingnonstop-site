Typing accuracy: how to reduce mistakes and fix your weak words

Better accuracy is the quickest way to raise your net speed, and it is easier than it sounds because most typists repeat the same few mistakes.

Slow down to speed up

If your accuracy is below 95 percent, reduce your pace by ten percent for a few days. Net speed often rises because you stop losing time and points to errors.

Find your repeat mistakes

Keep a list of the words you get wrong. You will usually see a pattern: swapped letters, a particular finger, or a particular punctuation mark. A short list of 20 or 30 words often accounts for most of your errors.

Drill only what you miss

Practise those words in short, repeated bursts rather than re-reading whole passages. Ten focused minutes on your mistake list beats an hour of general typing.

Digits and punctuation

Numbers, brackets, semicolons and capital letters are the usual trouble spots in office and legal passages. Practise them separately, and take note of which finger makes the reach.

Measure the right thing

Track accuracy next to speed. If your speed goes up while accuracy falls, you are trading away your net speed, and the exam usually punishes that.

Frequently asked questions

What accuracy should I aim for?

Many tests need around 92 to 98 percent. Aim for 97 percent or better in practice so that nerves do not push you below the limit.

Why do I make the same mistakes?

Muscle memory repeats whatever you practise, including errors. Targeted drills replace the habit.

Should I use backspace?

Use it when it is cheap, and practise sometimes without it to build confidence.
