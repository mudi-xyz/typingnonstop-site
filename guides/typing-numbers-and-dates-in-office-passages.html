Typing numbers, dates and amounts accurately in office passages

Numbers look simple, but they are slower and less accurate to type than letters for most people, because the digits sit on the top row.

The formats you will meet

  • Dates: 31.01.2027, 12-08-2026, 14th September 2026.
  • Amounts: Rs. 1,45,860.50 (with Indian-style commas), $1,275.50, 450 million.
  • Codes: INV/2026/48291, C-30571, 5021-7743-9086.
  • Percentages and fractions: 18%, 12.5 per cent.

Techniques that help

Keep your eyes on the source text and use the finger nearest each digit: left pinky for 1, left ring for 2, left middle for 3, left index for 4 and 5, right index for 6 and 7, right middle for 8, right ring for 9 and right pinky for 0. After each reach, return to the home row.

Practise in short bursts

Spend five minutes a day on digits only, then five on mixed strings that combine letters, digits and symbols. Type slowly and accurately first. Speed comes after the reach is automatic.

Check what the test counts

In data entry a single wrong digit can make a record useless, so examiners may weigh accuracy heavily. Read the rules for your test and practise with the same kind of data.

Verify as you type

Glance at the amount you typed once the line is complete. Reading it back takes a second, and it catches swapped digits before they become mistakes.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use the number pad?

Only if your test allows it and you have practised on it. Otherwise train on the top row.

Why do I make more mistakes on numbers?

The number row is a longer reach and gets less practice than letters. Short digit drills fix it.

How do I type Indian-style amounts?

Type the commas exactly as shown in the source, for example 1,45,860.50, and practise them as a pattern.
