How long does it take to reach 40 WPM? A practical way to plan

Many clerk and data-entry tests ask for around 40 WPM, so people want to know how long it will take. The honest answer is that it depends on where you start and how you practise, but you can plan it sensibly.

Start by measuring

Take a three-minute test and note your net speed and accuracy. That is your starting point. If you are far below, focus first on accuracy and touch typing basics.

Set small milestones

Instead of aiming straight at 40, set steps of about five WPM, for example 20, 25, 30, 35 and 40. Move to the next step only when you can hold the current speed at 95 percent accuracy or better in two or three tests.

Practise daily and deliberately

Short daily sessions that include a warm-up, a drill on your weak words and one timed run work better than rare long sessions. Our 30-day plan and 15-minute routine show how to structure it.

Expect plateaus

Progress is rarely a straight line. You may stay at one speed for a week and then jump. Keep tracking your net speed and accuracy, and do not give up during a plateau.

Add a margin

Once you can type 40 WPM in practice, aim for 45 so that nerves on test day do not drop you under the line.

Frequently asked questions

Can I reach 40 WPM in a month?

Some people can, others take longer. It depends on your start and your practice. Use milestones and track your own progress.

What if I am stuck at 30?

Check your accuracy, drill your weak words and try a different style of practice, such as shorter timed bursts.

Is 40 WPM enough?

It covers many of the requirements we have seen, but always check your own advert.
