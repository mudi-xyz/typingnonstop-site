Why your typing test score is lower than in practice

Many candidates see the same pattern: a comfortable 45 WPM in practice, then 35 on the day of the test. The drop is normal, and most of it has fixable causes.

The common reasons

  • Nerves. Stress tightens your hands and makes you rush or freeze. Even a small rise in tension lowers accuracy, and net speed falls with it.
  • Unfamiliar text. Practice text is often easy and repetitive. Test passages may contain long words, names, numbers and punctuation.
  • A different keyboard. Key travel, spacing and noise change how your fingers land. See which keyboard to use.
  • Different rules. Some tests do not allow backspace, or count each error heavily. Practising with different rules gives a different score. Read about backspace rules.
  • Gross versus net. Your practice number may be gross speed, while the test counts net speed. Learn the difference in net versus gross WPM.
  • Short runs. A one-minute score hides fatigue. A five or ten-minute test is slower per minute.
  • Peak versus average. Your best practice run is not your average. Test day usually lands closer to your average, or below it.

How to close the gap

  1. Measure honestly. Use the average of your last five runs at the same length and the same rules as the exam, not your best.
  2. Practise on exam-style passages. Use text with numbers, capitals and punctuation, as in office passages with numbers and dates.
  3. Simulate the test. Once a week, sit a full-length test with a timer, no pauses and no help.
  4. Practise on another keyboard. Change keyboards once or twice before the day.
  5. Build a buffer. If the pass mark is 35 WPM, practise until your average is 42 to 45 so that a normal drop still passes.
  6. Rehearse your routine. Use the test-day checklist for sleep, posture and warming up.

When your score drops mid-test

If you make a few mistakes early, keep your rhythm. Stopping to think costs more seconds than the mistakes did. The guide on mistakes that cost points lists the habits to avoid.

A realistic expectation

A drop of a few words per minute on test day is typical, and a larger drop usually points to one of the causes above. If your gap is large, fix accuracy first, because that raises net speed more reliably than trying to type faster.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my typing speed lower in the real test?

Common causes are nerves, unfamiliar text, a different keyboard, different rules and fatigue over a longer test.

How much lower should I expect on test day?

A few words per minute is common. A bigger drop usually means accuracy, keyboard or rule differences that you can practise.

How high should my practice score be?

Aim for an average at least 7 to 10 WPM above the pass mark, measured at the same length and with the same rules as the test.
